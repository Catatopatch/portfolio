Regenerative Braking System for E-bikes & E-scooters

A bench-scale demonstrator that recovers kinetic energy during braking and stores it in a supercapacitor. My contributions spanned the full system schematic, physical build, component integration, and testing. Built with Damanpreet Basra and Sufyan Jamal.

Overview

What it does

Conventional friction brakes waste a vehicle's kinetic energy as heat. This system recovers that energy — during braking, the BLDC motor acts as a generator, converting mechanical energy back into electrical energy, which is rectified and stored in a supercapacitor via a buck converter.

The prototype runs from a 7.5 V / 6 A supply. The ESP32 controls the 3-phase inverter during motoring and switches the MOSFET sequence to enable regenerative braking on command. A 10 F supercapacitor serves as the energy storage element, with the buck converter regulating voltage to stay within the 2.7 V cell limit.

The inverter was constructed across three separate breadboards — one per phase — so each half-bridge could be independently built, wired, and verified before full integration. This also made troubleshooting easier during testing.

Result

What we achieved

Key subsystems were successfully validated in isolation — wiring integrity confirmed, gate driver inputs verified, and the ESP32 Hall sensor interface tested and functional. Full motor operation was not achieved within the project timeline due to breadboard current limitations causing gate drive failures across most MOSFETs.

MATLAB simulations predicted 2–5 J of recoverable energy per braking event and a measurable supercapacitor voltage rise. The design is theoretically sound — the primary next step is migrating to a PCB rated for the required current levels.
