3-Phase BLDC Motor — Designed & Built from Scratch

Designed and CAD-modeled a functional outrunner brushless DC motor in SolidWorks — rotor, stator, magnet geometry, and bearing housing — then 3D printed, wound, and tested it to 2000 RPM at rated conditions.

My contribution — CAD modeling

Rotor & stator design

The rotor and stator were my primary responsibility — modeled from scratch in SolidWorks based on the team's calculated dimensions, then exported to STL and 3D printed for assembly.

Rotor — isometric view

Stator — isometric view (two-piece assembly)

Rotor

The inner surface features magnet cutouts with a 0.2 mm clearance margin around each magnet — tight enough to maintain alignment, loose enough for clean fitment. One face was kept completely flat for reliable 3D print bed adhesion.

Stator

Split into two halves, each with a flat print face. Teeth shaped to achieve a 0.5 mm air gap, positioning coils as close to the magnets as possible. Tooth wall thickness set to 1 mm for target flux density. Bearing housings on both ends let the shaft pass through without contacting the stator body.

Result

Motor running at target speed

After assembly — gluing the 8 N42 magnets, winding 14 turns of copper wire per tooth in a wye configuration, and pressing the bearings — the motor ran under its own power at 12 V / 2.5 A with no hand start required.
